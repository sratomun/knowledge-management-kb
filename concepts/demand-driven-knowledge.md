# Demand-driven knowledge

## What it is
Demand-driven knowledge is the principle that knowledge articles should be created from actual demand — the real questions and issues people bring — rather than authored in advance against anticipated needs. Content is a by-product of interaction: it is captured just-in-time while solving, reflects the requestor's context and words, and earns its place through reuse. The approach contrasts with front-loaded documentation efforts, and its proponents tie it to reduced duplicated effort and to content that stays relevant because it tracks what is actually being asked.
