# Architecture Development Method

## What it is

An architecture development method is the iterative, phased process at the core of an enterprise-architecture framework that guides architects from an initial vision through the development of the business, information-systems, and technology architectures to implementation and ongoing change management. It gives EA work a repeatable cycle with a governing requirements activity at its center, so architecture is produced and maintained as a managed process rather than a one-off document.

## How sources treat it

- **[[togaf]]** _(standard · normative)_ — The core of the framework is the Architecture Development Method (ADM), an iterative, cyclic process for developing and managing an enterprise architecture ⟨TOGAF⟩ [gen]
- **[[togaf]]** _(standard · normative)_ — The ADM proceeds through a Preliminary phase and phases A through H: A Architecture Vision, B Business Architecture, C Information Systems Architectures, D Technology Architecture, E Opportunities & Solutions, F Migration Planning, G Implementation Governance, and H Architecture Change Management, with ongoing Requirements Management at the center ⟨TOGAF⟩ [gen]
- **[[togaf]]** _(standard · normative)_ — The Fundamental Content includes the ADM together with ADM Techniques and guidelines for applying it ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- **[[togaf]]** _(standard · normative)_ — The Architecture Content Framework and Content Metamodel define the architectural artifacts, deliverables, and building blocks (ABBs/SBBs) produced across the ADM ⟨TOGAF⟩ [gen]

## Where sources differ

Only the TOGAF Standard is cited here. Most of the ADM detail — the phase list and its placement of Requirements Management at the center — is marked [gen] in the source, drawn from general knowledge because the full normative text was not consulted; only the presence of the ADM as core content and the existence of ADM Techniques come from the public overview page.
