# Knowledge Management

## What it is
Knowledge management (KM) is the discipline of deliberately getting the right knowledge to and between the right people at the right time, and helping them use it, so that an organization creates value from what it collectively knows. It spans creating, identifying, capturing, organizing, sharing, finding, and applying knowledge, and it depends on more than technology — strategy, people and culture, process, content, and governance all bear on whether knowledge actually flows.

- **[[apqc-km-framework]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — positions developing a KM strategy and business case as a foundational step before process and technology decisions ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- **[[iso-30401]]** _(standard · normative)_ — provides a common framework and vocabulary so KM programs can be started and matured regardless of the KM solution implemented, with value creation from organizational knowledge as the central aim ⟨iso.org/standard/68683⟩

- **[[apqc-km-maturity]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — treats KM as something that progresses from immature, inconsistent, ad hoc activity toward mature, disciplined approaches aligned to strategic business imperatives ⟨apqc.org/resource-library/resource-listing/apqcs-levels-of-knowledge-management-maturity⟩

## Where sources differ
The sources approach KM from complementary angles rather than in outright conflict. [[apqc-km-framework]] describes KM operationally, as a flow of knowledge plus enablers, and stresses that program design and adoption — not technology — are the main barriers. [[iso-30401]] frames KM as a certifiable management system with a common framework and vocabulary. [[apqc-km-maturity]] casts KM developmentally, as a capability that matures through staged levels. Each emphasizes a different facet (process, governing system, maturity trajectory); the KB leaves these standing side by side.
