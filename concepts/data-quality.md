# Data Quality

## What it is

Data quality is the discipline of ensuring that data is fit for its intended uses — accurate, complete, consistent, timely, and trustworthy enough that people and systems can rely on it. It covers the measurement, monitoring, and improvement of data against defined expectations.

## How sources treat it

- **[[dama-dmbok2]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Treats Data Quality as one of the ten knowledge areas surrounding governance on the DAMA Wheel ⟨DAMA Wheel⟩
- **[[dama-dmbok2]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Metadata Management and Data Quality are treated as distinct knowledge areas underpinning trust and usability across the other functions ⟨DAMA Wheel⟩
- **[[dama-dmbok2]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Each knowledge area, Data Quality included, is described with a consistent context-diagram structure of goals, inputs, activities, deliverables, roles, tools/techniques, and metrics ⟨DAMA Wheel⟩
- **[[dama-dmbok2]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Positions data-management functions, and the trust they produce, in service of treating data as a valuable strategic asset integrated with business objectives ⟨dama.org⟩

## Where sources differ

Only DAMA-DMBOK2 is cited here, so no cross-source divergence is recorded. Note that where DMBOK2 frames data quality as a knowledge area underpinned by governance, the corpus's Data Mesh material frames quality accountability as shifting upstream to domain data-product owners; that contrast is developed on the [[data-product]] and [[federated-computational-governance]] pages rather than resolved here.
