# Top-Level Ontology Categories

## What it is

The top-level categories of an upper ontology are the handful of most general kinds into which every other entity is sorted — the highest branches of the tree that all domain classes ultimately hang beneath. A recurring high-level distinction is between things that persist through time while keeping their identity and things that unfold or happen over time; this split then organizes finer categories such as objects, qualities, roles, and processes.

## How sources treat it

- **[[bfo]]** _(standard · normative)_ — At the top of BFO, entities divide into two mutually exclusive, exhaustive categories: continuant (entities that persist through time while maintaining identity) and occurrent (entities that unfold or happen in time, e.g. processes) ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home [gen]⟩
- **[[bfo]]** _(standard · normative)_ — Continuants further divide into independent continuants (objects, their parts and aggregates), specifically dependent continuants (qualities, roles, dispositions, functions), and generically dependent continuants (e.g. information artifacts) ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home [gen]⟩
- **[[bfo]]** _(standard · normative)_ — Occurrents include processes, process boundaries, temporal regions and spatiotemporal regions ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home [gen]⟩
- **[[bfo]]** _(standard · normative)_ — These categories are given both an OWL rendering and a first-order-logic axiomatization, and are paired with the OBO Relation Ontology (RO) and Information Artifact Ontology (IAO) as relational and information-entity companions ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / fol.html⟩

## Where sources differ

Only [[bfo]] is cited here, so there is no cross-source disagreement to surface. Within the corpus, BFO's continuant/occurrent split exemplifies the philosophically explicit style of category-making described under [[upper-ontology]] — a marked contrast to the business-language primitives (person, organization, agreement) that [[gist]] adopts in place of such abstractions.
