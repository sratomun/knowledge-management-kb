# Knowledge creation (SECI)

## What it is
A model of how organizations create knowledge by converting between tacit and explicit forms through four modes — Socialization, Externalization, Combination, Internalization — conceived as a continuous spiral that amplifies knowledge across levels.

## How sources treat it
- **[[nonaka-seci]]** _(article · informational)_ — proposes the SECI spiral of four conversion modes (tacit→tacit, tacit→explicit, explicit→explicit, explicit→tacit) and distinguishes tacit from explicit knowledge ⟨Nonaka & Takeuchi 1995, SECI⟩ [gen]
- **[[iso-30401]]** _(standard · normative)_ — the SECI model provides the knowledge-conveyance basis for how a knowledge management system moves knowledge between tacit and explicit forms ⟨iso.org/standard/68683⟩ [gen]

## Where sources differ
Nonaka & Takeuchi present SECI as an originating theory of knowledge creation; ISO 30401 references the same conversion idea as background to a management-system standard. They are complementary, not conflicting.
