# AI risk management

## What it is
The practice of identifying, measuring, and treating the risks that AI systems pose across their lifecycle, so as to cultivate trustworthy AI. It is typically organized as repeatable functions applied iteratively rather than a one-time assessment.

## How sources treat it
- **[[nist-ai-rmf]]** _(whitepaper · normative)_ — organizes AI risk management around four functions — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage — with Govern as a cross-cutting culture/process function ⟨NIST AI 100-1, §Core⟩ [gen]
- **[[nist-ai-rmf]]** _(whitepaper · normative)_ — defines characteristics of trustworthy AI (valid/reliable, safe, secure/resilient, accountable/transparent, explainable, privacy-enhanced, fair) and treats risk as contextual with tradeoffs among them ⟨NIST AI 100-1, §Trustworthiness⟩ [gen]

## Where sources differ
The single source here is explicitly voluntary guidance; it complements binding regimes such as [[risk-based-ai-regulation]], which mandate rather than recommend.
