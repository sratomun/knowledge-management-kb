# FAIR Data

## What it is

FAIR data is data (and metadata) managed so that it is **F**indable, **A**ccessible, **I**nteroperable, and **R**eusable — with an emphasis on machine-actionability, i.e. the capacity of computational systems to find, access, interoperate, and reuse the data with none or minimal human intervention. FAIR is a set of guiding principles for data stewardship rather than a standard or a specific technology; it distinguishes three kinds of entities it applies to — data (any digital object), metadata about that object, and the infrastructure that indexes and serves it.

## How sources treat it

- **[[fair-principles]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Defines four foundational goals expanded into numbered sub-principles: Findable (F1–F4), Accessible (A1, A1.1, A1.2, A2), Interoperable (I1–I3), and Reusable (R1, R1.1, R1.2, R1.3) ⟨F1–R1.3⟩
- **[[fair-principles]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Findability requires that (meta)data be assigned a globally unique and persistent identifier, described with rich metadata, and registered or indexed in a searchable resource ⟨F1, F2, F4⟩
- **[[fair-principles]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Accessibility requires that (meta)data be retrievable by their identifier using a standardised, open, free, and universally implementable communications protocol, allowing authentication and authorisation where necessary, and that metadata remain accessible even when the data are no longer available ⟨A1, A1.1, A1.2, A2⟩
- **[[fair-principles]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Interoperability requires (meta)data to use a formal, accessible, shared, and broadly applicable language for knowledge representation, to use vocabularies that themselves follow FAIR principles, and to include qualified references to other (meta)data ⟨I1, I2, I3⟩
- **[[fair-principles]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Reusability, the ultimate goal, requires (meta)data to be richly described with a plurality of accurate and relevant attributes, released with a clear and accessible data usage license, associated with detailed provenance, and meeting domain-relevant community standards ⟨R1, R1.1, R1.2, R1.3⟩
- **[[fair-principles]]** _(whitepaper · informational)_ — Emphasises machine-actionability because humans increasingly rely on computational support given the growth in volume, complexity, and creation speed of data ⟨go-fair.org/fair-principles⟩

## Where sources differ

Only the GO FAIR principles source is cited here, so no cross-source divergence is recorded. The source frames FAIR as guidelines to improve reuse of digital assets rather than as a prescriptive standard or an implementation, and it deliberately spans data, metadata, and infrastructure rather than data alone.
