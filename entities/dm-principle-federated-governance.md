# Federated computational governance (data mesh principle)

## What it is
The fourth data mesh principle: a decision-making model federating domain data product owners and platform product owners under global interoperability rules that the platform executes automatically.

## Key facts
- A decision-making model led by the federation of domain data product owners and data platform product owners, with domain-local decision-making power, adhering to a set of global rules ⟨martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html, §Federated computational governance⟩
- Requires a governance model embracing decentralization and domain self-sovereignty, interoperability through global standardization, a dynamic topology, and automated execution of decisions by the platform ⟨martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html, §Federated computational governance⟩
- Global decisions exist to create interoperability and a compounding network effect through discovery and composition of data products ⟨martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html, §Federated computational governance⟩
- Domains model polysemes — data elements that cross the boundaries of multiple domains, such as a unified identification of a 'user' ⟨martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html, §Logical architecture: computational policies embedded in the mesh⟩
- Inverts centralized governance: certification of golden datasets is no longer relevant; domains apply quality assurance locally while complying with global standards and SLOs automated by the platform ⟨martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html, §Logical architecture: computational policies embedded in the mesh⟩
- Success is measured by the network effect — connections representing consumption of data on the mesh — rather than the number or volume of governed tables ⟨martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html, §Logical architecture: computational policies embedded in the mesh⟩
