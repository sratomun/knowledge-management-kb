# FAIR Guiding Principles (GO FAIR)

## Scope & purpose

The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship were published in *Scientific Data* in 2016. They provide guidelines to improve the **F**indability, **A**ccessibility, **I**nteroperability, and **R**euse of digital assets. The principles emphasise machine-actionability — the capacity of computational systems to find, access, interoperate, and reuse data with none or minimal human intervention — because humans increasingly rely on computational support to deal with data given the growth in volume, complexity, and creation speed of data. The principles refer to three types of entities: data (or any digital object), metadata (information about that digital object), and infrastructure.

## Structure

The principles are organized under four foundational goals, each expanded into numbered sub-principles:

- **Findable** — the first step in (re)using data is to find them; metadata and data should be easy to find for both humans and computers, with machine-readable metadata essential for automatic discovery (F1–F4).
- **Accessible** — once found, the user needs to know how the data can be accessed, possibly including authentication and authorisation (A1, A1.1, A1.2, A2).
- **Interoperable** — data usually need to be integrated with other data and to interoperate with applications or workflows for analysis, storage, and processing (I1–I3).
- **Reusable** — the ultimate goal of FAIR is to optimise reuse; metadata and data should be well-described so they can be replicated and/or combined in different settings (R1, R1.1, R1.2, R1.3).

## Key points

- F1. (Meta)data are assigned a globally unique and persistent identifier ⟨F1⟩
- F2. Data are described with rich metadata (defined by R1 below) ⟨F2⟩
- F3. Metadata clearly and explicitly include the identifier of the data they describe ⟨F3⟩
- F4. (Meta)data are registered or indexed in a searchable resource ⟨F4⟩
- A1. (Meta)data are retrievable by their identifier using a standardised communications protocol ⟨A1⟩
- A1.1 The protocol is open, free, and universally implementable ⟨A1.1⟩
- A1.2 The protocol allows for an authentication and authorisation procedure, where necessary ⟨A1.2⟩
- A2. Metadata are accessible, even when the data are no longer available ⟨A2⟩
- I1. (Meta)data use a formal, accessible, shared, and broadly applicable language for knowledge representation ⟨I1⟩
- I2. (Meta)data use vocabularies that follow FAIR principles ⟨I2⟩
- I3. (Meta)data include qualified references to other (meta)data ⟨I3⟩
- R1. (Meta)data are richly described with a plurality of accurate and relevant attributes ⟨R1⟩
- R1.1. (Meta)data are released with a clear and accessible data usage license ⟨R1.1⟩
- R1.2. (Meta)data are associated with detailed provenance ⟨R1.2⟩
- R1.3. (Meta)data meet domain-relevant community standards ⟨R1.3⟩
- The principles emphasise machine-actionability so that computational systems can find, access, interoperate, and reuse data with none or minimal human intervention ⟨go-fair.org/fair-principles⟩
- The principles refer to three types of entities — data (or any digital object), metadata, and infrastructure — e.g. F4 requires that both metadata and data are registered or indexed in a searchable resource (the infrastructure component) ⟨go-fair.org/fair-principles⟩
