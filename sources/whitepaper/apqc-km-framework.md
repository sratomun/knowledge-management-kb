# APQC Knowledge Management Framework

## Scope & purpose

> Note: APQC's full 'Understanding the APQC KM Framework' document is a gated download and was not consulted. Key points below come from (1) a secondary source — APQC's public knowledge-management pages — and (2) general knowledge of the APQC KM framework (the seven-step knowledge flow process and its enablers); they are not quoted from the gated primary.

## Structure

The APQC KM framework is organized around a **knowledge flow process** — the sequence by which knowledge moves to and between the right people at the right time — surrounded by a set of **enablers** that make the flow sustainable.

The knowledge flow process comprises seven steps:

1. **Create** — new knowledge is generated through work, experience, research, problem-solving, and collaboration.
2. **Identify** — critical knowledge (what must be known, and by whom) is recognized and prioritized, often via knowledge mapping.
3. **Collect** — knowledge is captured from people, teams, and systems into a reusable form.
4. **Organize / Review** — collected knowledge is structured, curated, validated, and kept current so it remains findable and trustworthy.
5. **Share** — knowledge is moved and transferred between people, teams, and systems (e.g., communities of practice, transfer methods).
6. **Access** — people can find and retrieve the knowledge they need when they need it.
7. **Use / Apply** — knowledge is applied in the flow of work to produce business outcomes, which in turn generates new knowledge (closing the loop back to Create).

Surrounding the flow are the **enablers** that sustain it:

- **Strategy** — a KM strategy and business case aligned to organizational goals.
- **People / culture** — roles, skills, incentives, and a culture of sharing and reuse.
- **Process** — repeatable KM approaches, methods, and workflows.
- **Content & IT** — content management, tools, platforms, and technology.
- **Governance & measurement** — sponsorship, governance, benchmarks, metrics, and ROI.

## Key points

- Knowledge Management is defined as a collection of systematic approaches to help information and knowledge flow to and between the right people at the right time ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- APQC has been at the forefront of KM since 1993, when former CEO Carla O'Dell helped establish KM as a vital business discipline ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- The framework is built around a knowledge flow process whose seven steps are Create, Identify, Collect, Organize/Review, Share, Access, and Use/Apply ⟨APQC KM Framework⟩
- Enabling a program requires attention beyond technology — strategy, people/culture, process, content & IT, and governance/measurement act as enablers around the flow ⟨APQC KM Framework⟩
- APQC frames the biggest KM barriers as rarely being the technologies chosen, but rather program design and adoption factors ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- Knowledge sharing is the practice of moving useful know-how from people, teams, and systems to the people who need it, and includes more than publishing documents ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- Developing a KM strategy and a business case is a foundational step APQC positions before process and technology decisions ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- Identifying and mapping critical knowledge is a distinct capability APQC calls out, addressing what knowledge is at risk (e.g., from retirements) and who holds it ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- Communities of practice are a core KM approach for connecting people and enabling knowledge sharing and reuse ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- APQC offers an Interactive KM Framework and capability-assessment tools to gauge every aspect of a KM program, from strategy through processes and technologies ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- Organizations are encouraged to choose the right KM approach for their context rather than adopting a single prescribed method ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- KM program maturity is measured through benchmarks and metrics, including demonstrating return on investment from KM ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- Critical knowledge transfer and retention are emphasized to reduce knowledge loss during retirements, turnover, and role changes ⟨apqc.org/expertise/knowledge-management⟩
- The Use/Apply step closes the loop by generating new knowledge from application, feeding back into Create ⟨APQC KM Framework⟩
