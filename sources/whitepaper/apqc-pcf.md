# APQC Process Classification Framework (PCF)

## Scope & purpose

The APQC Process Classification Framework (PCF) is a taxonomy of cross-industry business
processes: a hierarchically grouped list of the key processes an organization performs,
used to objectively track and compare performance internally and externally against
organizations from any industry. It is the world's most widely used business process
taxonomy, serving as a common language and a basis on which to build benchmarking,
content management, and process management work rather than starting from scratch.

> Note: Only the public APQC landing page was available; the full PCF taxonomy (the
> complete list of categories, process groups, processes, activities, and tasks) is a
> gated Excel/PDF download. The key points below are summarized from the public page plus
> general knowledge of the PCF, not from the gated download itself.

## Structure

The PCF organizes business processes into a five-level hierarchy, moving from broadest to
most granular:

- **Category** — the highest level (Level 1); the PCF's top-level groupings of process.
- **Process Group** — Level 2; related processes grouped under a category.
- **Process** — Level 3; an individual end-to-end process.
- **Activity** — Level 4; the key events/steps performed when executing a process.
- **Task** — Level 5; the most granular, often organization-specific, unit of work.

The cross-industry PCF is conventionally organized around 13 top-level categories, split
between operating (value-creation) processes and management-and-support services
processes. APQC also publishes industry-specific PCF variants that add the processes and
nuances of a given industry on top of the cross-industry base.

## Key points

- The PCF is a taxonomy of business processes that lets organizations objectively track and compare performance internally and externally with organizations from any industry ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- Process frameworks are lists of all the key processes performed in an organization, grouped hierarchically to show how they relate to each other ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The PCF was developed in 1992 and is described as the world's most widely used business process framework ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The framework is used by hundreds of leading companies worldwide as a reliable and robust reference ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The PCF creates a common language so organizations can improve process management ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The latest cross-industry release referenced on the page is the Cross-Industry PCF Excel version 8.0, downloadable in Excel or PDF ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- APQC distinguishes between a cross-industry PCF and industry-specific PCFs that include industry-specific processes and nuances ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The PCF forms the basis for a variety of projects related to business processes ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- APQC identifies three primary ways organizations use the PCF to create business value: benchmarking, content management, and process management ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- APQC's Jonathan Kraft, Manager of Open Standards Benchmarking, describes five use cases for the PCF ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The PCF contains hundreds of processes, each tied to APQC-authored process definitions ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- Each process category has its own document with detailed definitions and key measures, aligning activities with global terminology ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The definition documents provide key performance indicators (KPIs) APQC recommends for measuring and tracking the performance of each process group ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
- The APQC PCF LinkedIn Community has nearly 3,000 members who share best practices around PCF use ⟨apqc.org/process-frameworks⟩
