# European Legislation Identifier (ELI)

## Scope & purpose

The European Legislation Identifier (ELI) is a standard that makes it easier to identify and describe legislation across Europe, ensuring legal documents can be accessed and used by public authorities, professionals, academics, businesses, and individuals ⟨Overview⟩. It aims to enable seamless access to legislation across borders and to make it understandable to both humans and machines ⟨Overview⟩. This page summarizes the "What is ELI?" overview published by the Publications Office of the EU on EUR-Lex ⟨URL⟩. ELI is managed by the Publications Office of the European Union ⟨Managed by⟩.

## Structure

- Standardised identification — a uniform way to manage and navigate legal documents ⟨Key features⟩
- Metadata description — a common vocabulary for metadata about national and EU legislation ⟨Key features⟩
- Cross-border compatibility — accessibility and interoperability across countries ⟨Key features⟩
- Four pillars — Identification (URI), Description (metadata), Publication (machine-readable format), Synchronisation (identifier lists/notifications) ⟨4 pillars⟩

## Key points

- ELI provides standardised identification, giving a uniform way to manage and navigate legal documents ⟨Key features⟩.
- ELI uses a common vocabulary to represent metadata about national and EU legislation, covering aspects such as purpose, scope, relationships, and legal basis ⟨Key features⟩.
- ELI is designed for cross-border compatibility, so legislation is easily accessible and interoperable across different countries ⟨Key features⟩.
- ELI is built on four pillars that may be implemented together or progressively ⟨4 pillars⟩.
- Pillar 1 (Identification): every legal text is assigned a universal resource identifier (URI) that lets users identify and access legislation consistently, usable in documents or published online ⟨Pillar 1⟩.
- Pillar 2 (Description): metadata is added to the legal text describing aspects such as the type of legislation, when it was adopted, and the jurisdiction it is subject to ⟨Pillar 2⟩.
- Pillar 3 (Publication): legal texts are published in a format that can be distributed, reused, and understood by computers ⟨Pillar 3⟩.
- Pillar 4 (Synchronisation): a list of all identifiers of legal texts can be provided to ELI users, who can be notified when a new identifier is created so they can keep their data up to date ⟨Pillar 4⟩.
- Implementing the four pillars, even gradually, provides flexible and transparent access to legislation ⟨Implementing⟩.
- When metadata is embedded in official journals, legal gazettes, or legal information systems, information can be exchanged automatically and efficiently by computers and humans ⟨Implementing⟩.
- ELI is managed by the Publications Office of the European Union ⟨Managed by⟩.
