# TOGAF Standard (The Open Group Architecture Framework)

## Scope & purpose

> Note: the full normative text of the TOGAF Standard is behind registration/download on The Open Group's site and was not consulted. Key points below come from (1) the public overview page (opengroup.org/togaf) and (2) general knowledge of the standard; points beyond the public page are marked [gen] and are not quoted from the primary text.

The TOGAF Standard, a standard of The Open Group, is a proven Enterprise Architecture (EA) methodology and framework used by leading organizations to improve business efficiency. It aims to ensure consistent standards, methods, and communication among Enterprise Architecture professionals, and to help practitioners avoid lock-in to proprietary methods, use resources more effectively, and realize a greater return on investment. It is used across small, medium, and large commercial businesses, government departments, non-government public organizations, and defense agencies, and supports a broad range of use-cases including agile enterprises and Digital Transformation.

## Structure

The TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition is designed around a dichotomy of common universal concepts and variable detailed configuration. It is divided into two parts: the TOGAF Fundamental Content, which provides the core concepts and practices, and the TOGAF Series Guides, which advise on how to configure the Fundamental Content for a given Enterprise Architecture practice. The stated intent is to separate enduring universal concepts and proven best practice from newer emerging ideas, so organizations can adapt the standard to their own configured EA practice.

The Fundamental Content is conventionally organized around several major components: the Architecture Development Method (ADM) — the core iterative process for developing an enterprise architecture; the ADM Techniques and guidelines for applying it; the Architecture Content Framework (including the Content Metamodel) describing the architectural artifacts, deliverables, and building blocks produced; the Enterprise Continuum and tools for classifying and reusing architecture assets; and reference models and an architecture capability framework. [gen] The related ArchiMate Specification (a separate standard of The Open Group) provides a modeling language for Enterprise Architecture. The predecessor Version 9.2 updated Version 9.1 with improved guidance and revisions to the Business Architecture and the Content Metamodel.

## Key points

- The TOGAF Standard is a proven Enterprise Architecture methodology and framework used by leading organizations to improve business efficiency. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- It is positioned as a prominent EA standard ensuring consistent standards, methods, and communication among Enterprise Architecture professionals. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- The approach helps practitioners avoid being locked into proprietary methods, use resources more efficiently, and realize a greater return on investment. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- The TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition is divided into the TOGAF Fundamental Content (core concepts and practices) and the TOGAF Series Guides (configuration guidance). ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- The standard is designed for the dichotomy of common universal concepts and variable detailed configuration, letting organizations adapt it to their configured EA practice. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- It combines universal concepts, best-practice guidance, and emerging ideas, and supports use-cases including agile enterprises and Digital Transformation. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- It is used by commercial businesses of all sizes, government departments, non-government public organizations, and defense agencies. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- The core of the framework is the Architecture Development Method (ADM), an iterative, cyclic process for developing and managing an enterprise architecture. [gen] ⟨TOGAF⟩
- The ADM proceeds through a Preliminary phase and phases A through H: A Architecture Vision, B Business Architecture, C Information Systems Architectures, D Technology Architecture, E Opportunities & Solutions, F Migration Planning, G Implementation Governance, and H Architecture Change Management, with ongoing Requirements Management at the center. [gen] ⟨TOGAF⟩
- TOGAF organizes architecture work into four architecture domains: Business, Data, Application, and Technology architecture (Data and Application together forming the Information Systems Architectures). [gen] ⟨TOGAF⟩
- The Architecture Content Framework, together with the Content Metamodel, defines the architectural artifacts, deliverables, and building blocks (ABBs/SBBs) produced across the ADM. [gen] ⟨TOGAF⟩
- The Enterprise Continuum provides a model for classifying, storing, and reusing architecture and solution assets, ranging from generic Foundation Architectures to organization-specific architectures. [gen] ⟨TOGAF⟩
- The TOGAF Library and TOGAF Series Guides supply supporting reference material and configuration guidance around the Fundamental Content. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
- A TOGAF certification portfolio (built on Version 9.2 and the 10th Edition) certifies individuals against the standard; the standard itself is maintained by The Open Group. ⟨opengroup.org/togaf⟩
