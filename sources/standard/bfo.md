# Basic Formal Ontology (BFO)

## Scope & purpose

- BFO is "a small, upper level ontology that is designed for use in supporting information retrieval, analysis and integration in scientific and other domains" ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- It is positioned as a "genuine upper ontology" — a domain-neutral, top-level ontology rather than a domain vocabulary ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- Because it is a genuine upper ontology, "it does not contain physical, chemical, biological or other terms which would properly fall within the coverage domains of the special sciences" ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- It is intended as a shared top-level backbone that domain ontologies extend, so that independently built vocabularies remain integrable and interoperable [gen].
- BFO is standardized as Part 2 of ISO/IEC 21838, "Top-level ontologies (TLO)": ISO/IEC 21838-2:2021 specifies BFO itself; ISO/IEC 21838-1 gives the requirements a top-level ontology must meet [gen].

## Structure

- The current normative version is BFO 2020, aligned with the ISO/IEC 21838-2:2021 standard; the source materials live under the BFO-2020 GitHub project ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home → GitHub github.com/bfo-ontology/BFO-2020⟩.
- BFO is offered in multiple axiomatizations: an OWL rendering and a first-order logic (FOL) rendering, the latter published on the project's "FOL" page ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / fol.html⟩.
- At the top of BFO, entities divide into two mutually exclusive, exhaustive categories: continuant (entities that persist through time while maintaining identity) and occurrent (entities that unfold or happen in time, e.g. processes) [gen].
- Continuants further divide into independent continuants (e.g. objects, their parts and aggregates), specifically dependent continuants (e.g. qualities, roles, dispositions, functions), and generically dependent continuants (e.g. information artifacts) [gen].
- Occurrents include processes, process boundaries, temporal regions and spatiotemporal regions [gen].
- BFO is paired with the OBO Relation Ontology (RO) and the Information Artifact Ontology (IAO) as its relational and information-entity companions; both appear among BFO users ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / users.html⟩.

## Key points

- The BFO project was initiated in 2002 under the "Forms of Life" project sponsored by the Volkswagen Foundation ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- The theory behind BFO was developed first by Barry Smith and Pierre Grenon and presented in a series of publications ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- Major contributions since then were made above all by Alan Ruttenberg, and by many others including Werner Ceusters, Chris Mungall, Fabian Neuhaus, James Overton, Bjoern Peters, Stefan Schulz and Barry Smith's wider group ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- BFO "is used by more than 550 ontology-driven endeavors throughout the world" ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- The Users page enumerates a large alphabetical directory of BFO-conformant ontologies spanning biomedicine, manufacturing, materials science, defense/intelligence, geology and the humanities ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / users.html⟩.
- Core OBO Foundry reference ontologies listed as BFO users include the Gene Ontology (GO), Chemical Entities of Biological Interest (ChEBI), Cell Ontology, Uber Anatomy Ontology (UBERON), Ontology for Biomedical Investigations (OBI), Phenotypic Quality Ontology (PATO), Protein Ontology (PRO) and Sequence Ontology (SO) ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / users.html⟩.
- BFO also underpins non-biomedical suites such as the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), the Industrial Ontologies Foundry (IOF) Core Ontology and the European Materials Modeling Ontology (EMMO) ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / users.html⟩.
- Named institutional and industry adopters include Boeing, AstraZeneca, Roche, Raytheon Technologies, Thermo Fisher Scientific, the U.S. Geological Survey, Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Lab and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / users.html⟩.
- BFO is authoritatively presented in the guidebook "Building Ontologies with Basic Formal Ontology," published by MIT Press ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home → Guidebook⟩.
- Supporting materials include a publications list, a first-order-logic axiomatization page, tutorials, a YouTube channel, user forums, a release-history page and a Portuguese translation ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
- ISO/IEC 21838-2:2021 is published jointly by ISO and IEC through their joint technical committee, giving BFO the status of an international standard top-level ontology [gen] ⟨basic-formal-ontology.org / Home⟩.
